**Không chấm Pronunciation.** Đây là bản gỡ băng, không có file âm thanh, nên
tiêu chí này không có dữ liệu. Cột đồng hồ dưới mỗi lượt là thời lượng nói tính
được, dùng để tham khảo khi chấm FC; dữ liệu ngắt nghỉ thật không có.

It also teaches me to appreciate the simple pleasures – a piece of mooncake, a sip of tea, the glow of lanterns – and to cherish the moments we share together. In a busy life, the Mid‑Autumn Festival is a beautiful pause that brings us all together under the same moon.

In my view, those moments help strengthen social bonds, because we all take part in the same tradition, and that can make a community more cohesive overall, especially in rapidly changing cities.

**Giám khảo:** You mentioned cleaning the house before the celebration; why do you think tidiness is linked to good luck?

**Thí sinh** _(0:38)_: In many cultures, a clean home is seen as a way to invite positive energy, so we believe that when the house is tidy, it clears away negativity and makes space for good fortune. My grandparents always say that a spotless floor reflects a clear mind, and that helps us start the new year on the right foot. It also shows respect for ancestors, because we want the spirits to feel comfortable in a well‑kept environment, which I think important for family harmony.

**Giám khảo:** What role does food, such as mooncakes, play in preserving cultural traditions?

**Thí sinh** _(0:37)_: Food is a strong symbol of heritage, mooncakes especially, they carry stories of reunion and history in every bite. When we share them, we also share the meanings behind the fillings—lotus paste, salty egg yolk—each representing different aspects of family unity. Preparing them together also passes skills from older generations to younger ones, so the recipes stay alive. In that sense, eating mooncakes becomes more than just a snack; it's a way to keep the culture alive, connecting past and present through taste.

**Giám khảo:** How might the practice of hanging colourful lanterns affect children’s perception of cultural identity?

**Thí sinh** _(0:37)_: Kids see the lanterns and instantly link them with the festival, so it creates a visual memory that sticks with them. The bright colours and shapes, especially the animal and star designs, make the tradition feel fun and exciting, rather than just a duty. When they carry a lantern around, they feel proud to be part of something special, which reinforces their sense of belonging to the community. Over time, those images become part of their identity, shaping how they view their heritage and its values.

**Giám khảo:** Do you think modern technology could change the way festivals like this are celebrated in the future?

**Thí sinh** _(0:38)_: Technology definitely has the potential to reshape celebrations, for example, people might use apps to order mooncakes online, or project virtual lanterns onto buildings, which could make the festival more accessible to those living abroad. However, I worry that too much digital involvement could dilute the personal touch, like the feeling of physically hanging a paper lantern with family. Still, if used wisely, tech can help preserve traditions by sharing them with a wider audience, ensuring the cultural significance stays relevant even as society evolves.
